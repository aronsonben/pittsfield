      LOCAL HISTORY / 04

      A small city with a long memory.

      From Berkshire farmland and summer visitors to a manufacturing city that built electrical machinery for much of the world — and is still repairing what that century left behind.

      1. SETTLEMENT / TOWN
        1761

        Incorporated as Pittsfield

        The township was incorporated in 1761 and named for William Pitt, the British statesman. It grew up around the Housatonic valley where the river slows and the farmland opens out — and by the 19th century it had become the shire town of Berkshire County, where the courts, the newspaper, and the county's civic life sat.

        • HOUSATONIC VALLEY FARMLAND
        • BERKSHIRE COUNTY SEAT / COURTHOUSE DISTRICT
        • WILLIAM PITT, EARL OF CHATHAM
      2. BOOKS & MOUNTAINS
        1850

        Melville at Arrowhead

        Herman Melville moved to a farm on Holmes Road in Pittsfield and named it Arrowhead. He wrote Moby-Dick there with Mount Greylock on the horizon — a reminder that this town's cultural history is not decoration on top of its working history, but part of the same story.

        • ARROWHEAD / HOLMES ROAD
        • MOBY-DICK WRITTEN IN THE BERKSHIRES
        • SUMMER VISITORS FROM THE CITIES BELOW
      3. THEATRES & MUSEUMS
        1903

        Colonial Theatre, Berkshire Museum

        Within two years of each other, the Colonial Theatre and the Berkshire Museum opened on South Street — a downtown built for shows, lectures, and a public that expected culture to be within walking distance. Both are still here, restored and running, which is rarer than it sounds for a city this size.

        • COLONIAL THEATRE / SOUTH STREET
        • BERKSHIRE MUSEUM / NATURAL HISTORY & ART
        • BARRINGTON STAGE COMPANY / DOWNTOWN
      4. MANUFACTURING
        1942

        The electrical plant and the transformer lines

        For most of the 20th century, General Electric's Pittsfield works made transformers, motors, and electrical equipment — and employed a large share of the city. The work also left contamination in Silver Lake and the Housatonic River, and one of the biggest river cleanups in New England has been running here ever since.

        • GE ELECTRICAL MANUFACTURING
        • HOUSATONIC RIVER PCB CLEANUP
        • REDEVELOPMENT OF PLANT SITES
      5. PARKS & WATER
        1975

        Onota, Pontoosuc and the state forest

        Two city lakes inside the municipal line, a state forest of more than eleven thousand acres on the ridge, and parkland along the river: the outdoors is not outside Pittsfield, it runs through it. Local traditions — the Fourth of July parade, the lakes in summer, the trails in October — are the ones residents actually mark their year by.

        • ONOTA LAKE / PONTOOSUC LAKE
        • PITTSFIELD STATE FOREST / BALANCE ROCK
        • FIRST STREET COMMON / DOWNTOWN PARK
      6. LITERATURE & LANDSCAPE
        1987

        Reading the county as a library

        Berkshire County keeps producing writers and readers, and the Pittsfield Athenaeum has been a working lending library since the 1870s. The county's political traditions are local too: town meeting, ward representation, open boards, and a public that shows up to argue about zoning in person.

        • PITTSFIELD ATHENAEUM
        • MAYOR & CITY COUNCIL / WARD REPRESENTATION
        • PUBLIC MEETINGS POSTED FOR THE COMMUNITY
