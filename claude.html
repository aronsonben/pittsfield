Town Services Directory

311

The 311 desk: how to report a problem

The city website lists no 311 number, so there is nothing to dial. Potholes, missed pickups, graffiti and similar problems go through PittSMART, on the web or as an iOS or Android app. You get an email when the request moves and can track it to the end. To talk to a person, call the City Hall main line.

Trash & Recycling Desk

The basics

  • Carts go out by 7 a.m. on collection day. Anything left beside a cart is not collected.
  • Each home gets two free 48-gallon carts, one for trash and one for recycling. Recyclables go in loose, with no bags.
  • Your street's day is on the 2026 recycling calendar or in the Casella app.
  • Yard waste goes to the Casella Transfer Station, 500 Hubbard Ave., with proof of residency.
  • Public Works 413-499-9330. Casella customer service 413-453-0098.

Holiday delay checker

When one of six holidays lands on a weekday, pickup for that day and every later day that week slips one day.

    Bulky item pickup

    Each item needs a $20 sticker from the City Clerk (70 Allen St.), Public Works (100 North St.), or Carr Hardware, Elm Street Hardware or Market 32. Put items out Monday of the pickup week, then call Casella to schedule.

      Pittsfield History

      The land was called Pontoosuck before it was Pittsfield. From a frontier plantation it grew through wool mills and electrical engineering into the seat of Berkshire County. Pick an era to filter the timeline.

        People worth knowing

        Rev. Thomas Allen

        Known as the "Fighting Parson" for his role in the Revolutionary War.

        Herman Melville

        Wrote Moby-Dick while living at Arrowhead, his Pittsfield farmhouse.

        William F. Bartlett

        Rose to major general during the Civil War at age 24, which the city calls the youngest in the war.

        Charles W. Whittlesey

        Earned the Medal of Honor commanding the "Lost Battalion" in World War I.

        Samuel Harrison

        Pittsfield pastor who pressed for equal pay for Black soldiers and served as chaplain of the 54th regiment.

        Culture & Getting Out

        In 2017 the Arts Vibrancy Index ranked Pittsfield and Berkshire County first among medium-sized communities in the country for the arts. Most of it is within a short walk of North Street.

        Colonial Theatre

        Opened in 1903 and still known for its acoustics.

        Barrington Stage Company

        Professional theatre with a downtown stage and a Musical Theatre Lab. barringtonstage.org

        Berkshire Museum

        Art, history and natural science in one downtown building. berkshiremuseum.org

        Berkshire Athenaeum

        The public library, with a Local History Department for deeper digging. pittsfieldlibrary.org

        Arrowhead

        Melville's home from 1850 to 1863, now a museum. mobydick.org

        Wahconah Park

        Professional baseball was played here from 1894 to 2003. The Pittsfield Suns have called it home since 2012.

        Pittsfield State Forest

        About 11,000 acres of trails, camping and a swimming beach.

        Onota and Pontoosuc Lakes

        Swimming, boating and fishing within city limits.

        Canoe Meadows

        A 264-acre Mass Audubon wildlife sanctuary along the Housatonic.

        Baseball, 1859

        Pittsfield hosted what is called the first intercollegiate baseball game. Amherst beat Williams 73 to 32.

        Music on South Mountain

        The Berkshire Music Festival began here in 1918 under Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge.

        Learning

        Pittsfield High, Taconic High, Miss Hall's School, and Berkshire Community College.

        City Government & Politics

        How the city is run

        Pittsfield uses a mayor-council form of government. The mayor serves a four-year term. Peter Marchetti took office in January 2024, and the next mayoral election is Tuesday, Nov 2, 2027. The eleven-member City Council has seven ward seats and four at-large seats.
