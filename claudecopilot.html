A Short History of Pittsfield

Before 1700s — The Mohican (Muh-he-con-ne-ok) people live, farm and fish in the upper Housatonic valley.
1750s — Settlers establish "Pontoosuc Plantation" around the Pontoosuc Lake and the Housatonic's west branch.
1761 — Incorporated as Pittsfield, named for British statesman William Pitt the Elder.
1791 — A town bylaw bans baseball within 80 yards of the new meeting house — one of the earliest written references to baseball in America.
1810 — Elkanah Watson holds the Berkshire Cattle Show here, a landmark in the American agricultural fair tradition.
1850–51 — Herman Melville lives at Arrowhead farm and writes Moby-Dick looking at Mount Greylock.
1891 — Pittsfield becomes a city.
1890s–1903 — William Stanley's electrical works arrive and become part of General Electric; Pittsfield becomes the "Electric City" building transformers.
1903 — Zenas Crane founds the Berkshire Museum.
1919 — Wahconah Park opens; baseball is still played there today.
1980s — GE downsizes dramatically; the city reinvents itself around healthcare, arts and small business. PCB contamination of the Housatonic leads to decades of cleanup.
2000s — The Colonial Theatre reopens (2006) and cultural tourism takes root downtown.

Dates summarized from general local history; consult the Berkshire Athenaeum's Local History collection for sources.

Arts & Letters

  • Berkshire Museum — art, natural history, an aquarium, since 1903.
  • Colonial Theatre — restored 1903 theatre on Pittsfield's Berkshire Theatre Group stage circuit.
  • Barrington Stage Company — Tony-honored regional theatre on Union Street.
  • Arrowhead — Melville's home, run by the Berkshire County Historical Society.
  • Berkshire Athenaeum — the public library, with Melville collections.

Out & About

  • Onota Lake & Pontoosuc Lake — boating, fishing, summer sunsets.
  • Pittsfield State Forest — trails, Berry Pond, the mountain laurel in June.
  • Springside Park — in-town woods and playgrounds.
  • Wahconah Park — wooden grandstand baseball under the evening sun.
  • Third Thursdays — downtown summer street festival on North Street.

Neighbors

Pittsfield sits between Tanglewood to the south, Jacob's Pillow to the southeast, MASS MoCA to the north, and the ski hills of the Berkshires all around. It is a working city with a strong labor heritage, a diverse community of Irish, Italian, Polish, French-Canadian and newer immigrant families, and a growing creative economy.

How the City Is Run

  • Form: Mayor-council city charter (since 1891).
  • Mayor: Elected citywide to a two-year term. Currently Peter Marchetti.
  • City Council: Eleven members — four at-large and seven by ward.
  • School Committee: Elected members plus the mayor as chair.
  • Elections: Municipal elections in odd-numbered years.

Verify current officeholders at cityofpittsfield.org.

Get Involved

  • City Council meets regularly at City Hall, 70 Allen Street; meetings are open to the public.
  • Register to vote through the state elections portal or the City Clerk.
  • Boards and commissions regularly seek volunteer residents.
  • Talk it over on the Forum Board.

Civic Themes

Recurring local conversations: downtown revitalization, the William Stanley Business Park, housing costs, Housatonic River cleanup, schools, and how to attract young families while honoring a proud industrial past. Weigh in on the board — politely, this is a small city.
